Scope: definitions of arithmetic mean and sample median; the usual median convention for an even number of observations; the different sensitivity of these summaries to extreme values. Fictional examples, in minutes:
A: 2, 3, 4, 5, 36. Sum 50; count 5; arithmetic mean 10; median 4.
B: 2, 3, 4, 5, 66. Sum 80; count 5; arithmetic mean 16; median 4.
C: 2, 4, 6, 20. Sum 32; count 4; arithmetic mean 8; median (4 + 6) / 2 = 5.
Combined groups: 2 people with mean 2, and 8 people with mean 12. Total 2*2 + 8*12 = 100; count 10; overall mean 10. The unweighted mean of the group means is 7 and answers a different question.
Self-check: 1, 2, 3, 4, 40. Sum 50; count 5; arithmetic mean 10; median 3.

Equal-sized groups' arithmetic means can be simply averaged to recover the overall arithmetic mean. Group medians and sizes alone generally do not determine the overall median without more ordering information.
